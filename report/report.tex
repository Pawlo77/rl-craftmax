\begin{abstract}
We compare three baseline agents on Craftax-Symbolic (PPO, PPO-RNN and PQN-RNN) with four additions to them: a count-based progress bonus, self-imitation, Q-learning over hand-written skills, and Dyna-Q on top of those skills. We also combine the two best additions. All agents train for 1M and for 1B environment steps with the same protocol, using 10 and 3 seeds. At 1M the progress bonus on PPO-RNN reaches \scoreOneMCountBonusPpoRnn\,\% against \scoreOneMPpoRnn\,\% for plain PPO-RNN, which is within the noise between seeds, and the skill methods end far below. At 1B the baselines change places: PQN-RNN goes from the weakest control to the level of the best one, no addition beats its own baseline by more than the noise, and the rank correlation between the two budgets is only \scaleSpearman. Rerunning the baselines on the cluster's software stack gives the same scores as on our laptop. We release the code, the results and a small demo.\footnote{Code: \url{https://github.com/Pawlo77/rl-craftmax}; results dataset: \url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/Pawlo77/wut-murl-craftax-results}; demo: \url{https://huggingface.co/spaces/Pawlo77/wut-murl-craftax}; videos: linked from the demo.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Craftax \cite{matthews2024craftax} is a survival game written in JAX, with 67 achievements spread over nine floors and a maximum reward of 226. Its scoreboard has two budgets, one million (1M) and one billion (1B) environment steps, and the published baselines are PPO variants and a recurrent PQN. We wanted to know which simple additions to such baselines actually help, and whether a result at 1M still holds when the budget is a thousand times larger. This report contributes:
\begin{itemize}
\item a comparison of three baselines, four additions and their combination, with the same protocol at both budgets;
\item an analysis of how the results change from 1M to 1B (rank agreement, gain over the baseline, learning speed, what three seeds can resolve), and a check that the software stack is not the reason;
\item decision trees fitted to every final policy;
\item the code, the results and a small demo, all released.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}

The Craftax paper \cite{matthews2024craftax} reports PPO, a recurrent PPO and three intrinsic-reward variants (RND, ICM, E3B). Recurrent PQN \cite{gallici2025simplifying} has the best published 1B score of the baselines we reproduce. We only compare numbers that were measured in the same way, that is the training score at the same budget. Self-imitation \cite{oh2018self} and Dyna-style planning \cite{sutton2018reinforcement} are older ideas, which we adapted to the achievement structure of Craftax. For the statistics we follow \cite{agarwal2021deep}: interquartile mean, bootstrap intervals and the probability of improvement.

\section{Methods}

\begin{description}
\item[Controls.] PPO \cite{schulman2017proximal} with an MLP policy; PPO-RNN, the same with a GRU for memory; and PQN-RNN, which does online Q($\lambda$) learning on the batch from many parallel environments, without a replay buffer or a target network. Each method is compared with its own base control, and only inside its block.
\item[Count-based progress bonus.] A bonus of $\beta_t/\sqrt{N[\phi(s_{t+1})]}$ is added to the reward. Here $\phi$ is a short summary of game progress (floor, tools, armour, spells, bucketed inventory) and $N$ counts the visits over the whole run. We use it on PPO-RNN and on PQN-RNN, and we also test a version that hashes the whole observation (SimHash).
\item[Self-imitation (SIL).] PPO-RNN with a buffer of the steps that led to each achievement. An extra loss copies a stored action only when the return that followed was higher than the critic expected \cite{oh2018self}.
\item[Skill methods (use hand-written skills).] SMDP Q-learning keeps a table over hashed progress keys and 25 hand-written options and learns it from the game reward, while one shared recurrent Q-network learns the skills themselves from success rewards. Dyna-Q adds a learned model of what each skill does and plans with it. Since the skills are given by hand, we report these two in a separate block.
\item[Combination.] The progress bonus and SIL together on PPO-RNN. They are the two best compatible methods by the rank rule that uses both budgets, and the check at 1B gave the same pair.
\end{description}

\section{Experimental setup}

\begin{description}
\item[Benchmark and budgets.] Craftax-Symbolic, 1M and 1B steps.
\item[Seeds.] 3 per configuration while tuning, 10 per final run at 1M and 3 at 1B.
\item[Tuning.] At 1M we tune in two stages, a coarse grid and then a finer one. The 1B runs reuse the 1M settings and the published 1B settings for the shared PPO part, so a method may look worse at 1B than it could with its own tuning.
\item[Score.] The mean return of the training episodes over the last 2\,\% of the budget (at least 100 episodes), as a share of the 226 points. We also evaluate 64 fixed seeds, stochastic and greedy, and report that next to it.
\item[Statistics.] Mean $\pm$ s.e., the interquartile mean (IQM) with 95\,\% bootstrap intervals over seeds, the probability of improvement (PoI), and Holm-corrected Mann--Whitney tests at 1M. With three seeds at 1B we give intervals and no tests.
\item[Hardware and software.] The 1M runs ran on a laptop (Apple silicon) and the 1B runs on an A100/H100 cluster. We used JAX 0.11.2, craftax 1.6.1 (1M) and 1.7.0 (1B), and MLflow for all metrics. Because budget and software differ at the same time, we reran the three controls at 1M on the cluster (10 seeds each).
\end{description}

\section{Results}

Table~\ref{tab:main} gives the scores.
\begin{description}
\item[At 1M.] PPO (\scoreOneMPpo), PPO-RNN (\scoreOneMPpoRnn), SIL (\scoreOneMSil) and the combination (\scoreOneMCombo) end very close to each other. The progress bonus on PPO-RNN has the highest mean (\scoreOneMCountBonusPpoRnn) but it is not significantly above PPO-RNN (PoI \poiOneMCountBonusPpoRnnPpoRnn, Holm $p=\pHolmOneMCountBonusPpoRnnPpoRnn$). The PQN-RNN family is lower (PQN-RNN \scoreOneMPqnRnn, PoI against PPO \poiOneMPqnRnnPpo, Holm $p=\pHolmOneMCountBonusPqnRnnPpo$ for its count-bonus variant). The skill methods are lowest (Dyna-Q \scoreOneMDynaQ, SMDP \scoreOneMSmdpSkills; Holm $p=\pHolmOneMDynaQPpoRnn$ and $\pHolmOneMSmdpSkillsPpoRnn$ against PPO-RNN), though not significantly below their own control PQN-RNN. In the greedy evaluation PPO-RNN does best (\evalGreedyOneMPpoRnn\ against \evalGreedyOneMPpo\ for PPO). No final evaluation episode reaches an iron pickaxe.
\item[At 1B.] All agents score several times higher and the order changes (Fig.~\ref{fig:scale}). PQN-RNN is now close to PPO-RNN (\scoreOneBPqnRnn\ against \scoreOneBPpoRnn), PPO is well behind (\scoreOneBPpo), and the skill methods are still last (Dyna-Q \scoreOneBDynaQ, SMDP \scoreOneBSmdpSkills). The 1M and 1B means of the nine methods have a Spearman correlation of \scaleSpearman\ (95\,\% CI \scaleSpearmanLo\ to \scaleSpearmanHi). The advantages from 1M do not carry over: the bonus on PQN-RNN gains \scaleGainOneMCountBonusPqnRnn\,\% over its control at 1M and \scaleGainOneBCountBonusPqnRnn\,\% at 1B, and SIL goes from \scaleGainOneMSil\,\% to \scaleGainOneBSil\,\% (a change of \scaleChangeSil, the only change whose interval excludes zero). With three seeds a gap below about one point cannot be resolved, which does not mean the two methods are equal. Table~\ref{tab:repro} compares the controls with the published scores. All of them are inside the acceptance band except PPO-RNN at 1B, which is \gateGapOneBPpoRnn\ above the published \gatePubOneBPpoRnn\,\%; we did not find out why. PPO, PPO-RNN and PQN-RNN stop improving in the last fifth of the budget on all three seeds, while SIL and the combination do not. The share of the budget that a method needs to reach 90\,\% of its final score also moves for most methods (Fig.~\ref{fig:scale}, right). Table~\ref{tab:abl} lists the ablations at 1M: the SimHash variant of the progress bonus (\scoreOneMCountBonusPpoRnnSimHash) and every ablation of the skill methods are below their full versions, and the buffer layout of SIL makes no difference (\scoreOneMSilSingleBuffer\ against \scoreOneMSil). At 1B the SimHash variant stops after a few million steps because its hash table fills up. The other 1B ablations were still running when we wrote this.
\end{description}

\section{Interpretation}

We fitted decision trees (16 to 4096 leaves) to every final policy. No tree agrees with its network on 90\,\% of the states. At 256 leaves the trees agree more with the 1B networks than with the 1M ones (PPO-RNN: \fidTwoFiftySixOneMPpoRnn\ at 1M, \fidTwoFiftySixOneBPpoRnn\ at 1B), but when they act themselves they keep only a small part of the network's score (closed-loop ratio of PPO-RNN \closedLoopOneBPpoRnn\ at 1B and \closedLoopOneMPpoRnn\ at 1M). So agreement on logged states does not say much about how a policy plays. The leading features of the trees also change with the budget. At 1M they are the previous action and the distances to water and trees. At 1B, for every method without skills, they are the facing direction, then whether the agent is asleep and whether the next tile is walkable, and a 16-leaf tree reads roughly as ``walk the way you face, act when blocked''. The skill methods rely on the previous action and on the block they face. Removing the memory features changes the 256-leaf fidelity only a little for the recurrent methods.

\section{Discussion and conclusion}

We expected the progress bonus to help the recurrent PPO. It does not: the gain is inside the noise at 1M and gone at 1B. The hand-written skills lower the score at both budgets, and clearly so at 1B. The main limits are the three seeds and the missing tuning at 1B, one environment version per budget, two GPU types at 1B, and the PPO-RNN gap that we could not explain. The software stack also differed between the 1M and 1B runs, but rerunning the controls on the cluster gave differences within the seed noise.
\begin{description}
\item[Reproducibility.] Every number and table in this report is generated from the MLflow store. Each run keeps its configuration, package versions and commit, and a 1B run takes a few hours on one GPU.
\item[Future work.] Tuning the methods at 1B, more 1B seeds so that close methods can be ordered, a learned instead of a hand-chosen progress summary, and the intrinsic-reward baselines of the Craftax paper on the same protocol.
\end{description}
