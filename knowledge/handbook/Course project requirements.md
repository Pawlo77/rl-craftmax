# Course project requirements

Source: the instructor's project description, pasted on 2026-11-01. The project is to develop and evaluate a reinforcement learning agent for a chosen game. Existing libraries and implementations may be used if they are acknowledged and the team can explain how the solution works. The project is meant as a portfolio piece (README, short report, presentation video).

## Grading (50 points, one shared score per team)

| Component | Points | Date | What is assessed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Checkpoint | 10 | 23 to 27 November 2026 | See the checkpoint table. |
| Implementation and correctness | 12 | 18 January 2027 | Correctness, scope of completed work, reproducibility; a well-organised GitHub repository with a README that introduces the project and explains setup and execution. |
| Methodology and experiments | 10 | 18 January 2027 | A research question or clear objective; justified choices, appropriate baselines and metrics, fair comparisons, handling of randomness, sound interpretation. |
| Presentation and discussion | 14 | 25 January 2027 | Clear explanation of project and findings (6), understanding shown in the questions (6), readable materials and time management (2). |
| Report | 4 | 18 January 2027 | A concise summary of objective, approach, findings and limitations, with plots of the most important observations. |

- Passing needs 25 of 50 in total; there is no minimum per component. A team with 0 of 10 at the checkpoint can still pass with 25 of the other 40.
- A well-executed project with negative results can earn full marks: not beating a baseline does not lower the score; correctness, sound experiments and justified conclusions count.

## Checkpoint (meeting with the instructor, in person or remote)

| Points | For |
|---|---|
| 2 | A clearly defined problem and objective, and a GitHub repository the instructor can access. |
| 4 | A working minimal experiment: running the environment and the agent or baseline, through to measuring performance. |
| 2 | Presenting and explaining an initial result, with its limitations. |
| 2 | A concrete plan for further experiments and the division of work. |

Strong performance is not required; the checkpoint grades the progress made by the meeting.

## Deliverables

- **Report:** LaTeX, in the repository as `.tex` source and compiled `.pdf`; at most 3 pages including text, figures and tables, references excluded. Setup and execution details go in the README, not the report.
- **Presentation:** 15 minutes plus 7 minutes of questions, either a standard in-person talk or a YouTube video shown in the team's slot. In both cases the team attends the session in person and joins the live discussion.
- **Discussion:** questions may go to any team member; everyone should explain their own contribution and how it fits the project.

## How the plans follow it

- The report is planned at 3 pages with the references after them; the disclosure and the acknowledgements sit inside the 3 pages (decision of 2026-11-01 in [[Experiment decisions]], plan [[2026-10-20-craftax-report]]). Confirm with the instructor that this is the reading of "references excluded" if the disclosure should not count.
